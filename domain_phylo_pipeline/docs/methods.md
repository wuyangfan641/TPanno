# Methods (manuscript-ready text)

> The paragraphs below follow the default parameters of this pipeline. When preparing a manuscript, replace the angle-bracketed placeholders `<...>` with your sample count, HMM source, software versions, and actual parameter values. All values are recorded in `results/06_report/parameters.tsv` and `software_versions.txt`.

## 1. Sequence data and domain identification

Starting from the 27 predicted proteomes (one protein FASTA per sample), input quality control was performed with a custom Python script. Terminal stop characters were removed, internal stop characters and non-standard amino acids were recoded as `X` by default; U and O residues were converted to `X` by default (with an option to retain them), and sequences shorter than 30 aa or with more than 50% `X` residues were discarded. Analysis was aborted when duplicate sequence IDs were found within a file, and the IDs were required to be corrected before rerunning. Each proteome was then searched against Pfam-A profile HMMs with HMMER 3.3.2, using either `hmmsearch` or `hmmscan` under the **Pfam gathering threshold (`--cut_ga`)** when GA thresholds were available; otherwise an E-value threshold of 1e-3 was used. The gathering threshold is a manually curated compromise between sensitivity and specificity and is preferable to an arbitrary E-value cutoff. For every significant hit, the aligned region was required to cover at least 70% of the HMM model length (`(hmm_to - hmm_from + 1) / qlen >= 0.70`), the domain had to be at least 30 aa long, and the `X` fraction within the domain had to be at most 30%. To avoid treating multiple domain copies from the same protein as independent taxonomic units, only the domain copy with the **highest HMM coverage** was retained per protein per family; ties were broken by i-Evalue and then by bit score. Tandem duplicates were recorded separately and can be retained in full for sensitivity analyses.

Domain subsequences were extracted using the HMMER `ali` coordinates (not the full protein and not the `env` envelope), so that the phylogenetic signal comes strictly from the homologous region modeled by the HMM. This avoids interference from non-homologous N- and C-terminal segments, signal peptides, and low-complexity regions in the multiple sequence alignment.

### 1.1 Family screening in full-Pfam scans

When `hmmscan` is used to screen the entire Pfam-A database, all candidate domain families were first extracted with lenient thresholds (at least 4 sequences and at least 1 sample). An independent script then applied the following filters: the number of samples in which the family occurred (with 27 species, >= 14 corresponds to most species and >= 22 to >= 80% conservation), the number of domain sequences, the median HMM coverage (>= 0.75), the median domain length (>= 30 aa), the median number of copies per sample, and the fraction of single-copy samples. The last two metrics diagnose paralogy: families with a low single-copy fraction can still be clustered as domain families, but they must not be interpreted directly as orthogroups. When `Pfam-A.clans.tsv` was available, only the highest-scoring HMM within each clan was retained, preventing the same domain from being represented by several overlapping models. Families were finally ranked by a composite score combining sample coverage, single-copy fraction, and sequence count, and the first round of tree inference was limited by `MAX_FAMILIES` (for example, 300). It should be emphasized that a full-library `hmmscan` is an **HMM-guided survey of known domains**, not unsupervised clustering from sequence data; families absent from Pfam do not appear in the results.

## 2. Multiple sequence alignment and trimming

For each family, the domain sequences were aligned with MAFFT 7.526. When the number of sequences was <= 200, L-INS-i was used (`--localpair --maxiterate 1000`), an algorithm that offers a good balance between accuracy and runtime for small and medium-sized families. When more than 200 sequences were present, `--auto` was used, allowing MAFFT to choose among G-INS-i, L-INS-i, and FFT-NS-2 according to data size and avoiding the O(n^2) memory and time cost of L-INS-i. Alignments were trimmed with trimAl 1.4 in `-gappyout` mode, which determines the gap threshold from the overall gap distribution and is more robust to heterogeneity in indel patterns among families than a fixed `-gt` cutoff. Families with fewer than 30 columns after trimming were not used for tree inference; families retaining less than 30% of the original alignment columns triggered a warning.

## 3. Phylogenetic inference

Maximum-likelihood trees were inferred for each trimmed alignment with IQ-TREE 2.3.6. The substitution model was selected by ModelFinder Plus (`-m MFP`) according to the Bayesian information criterion (BIC) among candidate amino-acid models; the model was chosen independently for each family rather than applying a single model to the whole dataset. Branch support was assessed with 1000 ultrafast bootstrap replicates (`-B 1000`) together with `-bnni` to correct UFBoot overconfidence under model misspecification, and with 1000 SH-aLRT replicates (`-alrt 1000`). The random seed was fixed at 12345. All trees were kept unrooted by default: clustering is based on patristic distances on the unrooted tree and does not depend on a rooting assumption. Trees were rooted with `-o <outgroup>` only when a reliable outgroup was available. A branch was considered to have strong topological support when UFBoot was >= 95 and SH-aLRT was >= 80; branches with insufficient support were not used to delimit subfamilies.

## 4. Tree-based clustering

For each family tree, patristic distances were computed between all pairs of terminal nodes (that is, the expected number of substitutions per site), and complete-linkage hierarchical clustering was used to cut the dendrogram at a threshold of t = 0.50 substitutions per site, yielding candidate subfamilies (clusters). Complete linkage was chosen because it guarantees that the patristic distance between any two sequences in the same cluster does not exceed t (that is, the cluster diameter is bounded), making it less prone than average linkage to chaining across large divergences; this definition is also close to the cluster-diameter objective of TreeCluster. Patristic distance was preferred over raw sequence identity because the tree explicitly models multiple substitutions, site-rate heterogeneity, and the amino-acid replacement matrix; patristic distance is therefore consistent with the tree topology and branch lengths and is more robust to saturation among distantly related sequences. To assess the robustness of the threshold, clustering was repeated over a grid from t = 0.10 to 1.00, and the number of clusters, singleton fraction, and mean silhouette were reported for each threshold. The final threshold should lie on a plateau of the silhouette curve and should be interpreted together with independent evidence such as domain architecture, gene synteny, expression, or functional experiments, rather than treating a single threshold as an objective species or subfamily boundary. For each cluster, the pipeline reports sample composition, mean and maximum within-cluster distance, minimum between-cluster distance, silhouette, monophyly, and UFBoot/SH-aLRT support. When very large gene families were dereplicated with CD-HIT at 99% identity, cluster numbers were mapped back to all members and representative sequences were marked in the results.

## 5. Software and reproducibility

HMMER 3.3.2; MAFFT 7.526; trimAl 1.4.rev22; IQ-TREE 2.3.6; CD-HIT 4.8.1; Python 3.11; Biopython 1.83; NumPy/SciPy. All parameters are centralized in `config/config.sh`, the IQ-TREE random seed is fixed, software versions are written to `results/06_report/software_versions.txt`, and the coordinates and E-values of every domain are written to `metadata.tsv`. The pipeline supports resuming from checkpoints.

## 6. Recommended sensitivity analyses

1. **Trimming strategy:** set `TRIMAL_MODE` to `automated1`, rerun, and compare topology and support.
2. **Alignment algorithm:** set `MAFFT_MODE` to `einsi` (useful when domain boundaries differ substantially), rerun, and compare.
3. **Clustering threshold:** select two or three thresholds from `cutoff_scan.tsv` and report the change in cluster number and the stable clusters.
4. **Domain copies:** set `KEEP_ALL_DOMAINS=1` and check whether tandem duplicates change the subfamily assignment.
5. **Dereplication:** run very large gene families with CD-HIT at 95% and 99% identity and confirm that cluster composition is unchanged.
6. **Long branches:** inspect clusters with unusually large `mean_within`, and if necessary remove long-branch sequences or rerun with a site-heterogeneous model (for example, `LG+C20+F+G`).
